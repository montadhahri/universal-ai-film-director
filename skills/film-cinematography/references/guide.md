# Shot craft

Camera: wide establishes geography; medium connects action; close-up expresses detail or choice. Each movement must reveal, follow or contrast an action. Maintain screen direction and eyeline between cuts. Use intentional changes of axis.

Timing table: shot ID, start, end, duration, visual action, shot size/movement, transition, audio cue, continuity references, planned state. Use two distinct time models: **global edit intervals** (the on-screen film timeline, whose union must cover the target without unintended gaps or double-counting) and **source-clip durations** (which may overlap during a dissolve). For nonoverlapping source clips, total edit duration equals the sum of clip durations. For a simple sequential chain with declared dissolves, total edit duration equals the sum of source durations minus overlap durations. Do **not** subtract overlaps again from already-global edit intervals. Record each transition's incoming/outgoing shot IDs, overlap duration and edit-time interval. Check both models agree; if timing is underspecified, mark UNRESOLVED rather than inventing coverage.

Keep lighting physically motivated and consistent with the approved visual bible. Do not assume a generator can perform an exact dolly or maintain a reference identity without verified testing.
