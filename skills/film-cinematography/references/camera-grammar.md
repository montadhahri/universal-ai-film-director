# Cinematography — practical shot grammar

This reference is creative guidance, not a camera or video-generation API.

## Shot sizes and visual meaning

- Extreme wide: place, isolation, scale; often weaker for facial detail.
- Wide/full: body movement, geography and action.
- Medium: performance, gesture, dialogue.
- Close-up: facial emotion, critical prop or story reveal.
- Extreme close-up: a very specific detail; requires visual context.

## Angle and perspective

- Eye level: neutral, intimate viewpoint.
- High angle: context or vulnerability; not automatically a power statement.
- Low angle: scale or dominance; avoid unmotivated dramatic framing.
- Over-the-shoulder: relationships and eyeline structure.
- POV: subjective attention; maintain established spatial geography.

## Motion and edit continuity

- Static locked camera is often the most reliable way to preserve readable
  action and character proportions in generative video.
- Pan follows or reveals horizontally; tilt vertically; tracking/dolly moves
  through space; zoom changes focal length rather than moving the camera.
- Avoid conflicting simultaneous moves unless essential and generator-tested.
- Maintain screen direction, eye lines, horizon, subject position, and
  continuity of action across a cut.
- Match on action for smooth continuity; use deliberate jump cuts only when
  stylistically justified. Establish a new axis when crossing screen direction.
- Cut on a narrative or emotional change; a transition effect is not a
  substitute for a motivated edit.

## Director's decision check

For each camera choice record: purpose, framing, movement, action,
continuity risks, and what the following shot must preserve.
