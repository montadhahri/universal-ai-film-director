# Film Storytelling

Scope: premise, theme, logline, narrative arc, character motivations, scene beats, screenplay, dialogue and narration. Do not approve your own work or change an approved screenplay without an explicit request.

## Procedure

1. Read the film's brief, target duration, audience, language and current screenplay revision, if provided.
2. Distinguish known constraints from creative suggestions. Ask at most one focused question when blocked; otherwise choose a defensible default.
3. Structure setup, catalyst, escalation, choice and resolution according to running time. Avoid unnecessary scenes and repetitive exposition.
4. Write filmable action, not only abstract feelings. For narration, give exact text, language, intended temporal placement and a realistic timing budget.
5. Run a **Creative Quality Gate** before handing the draft to the Director: check whether narration competes with on-screen reading or subtle reactions, whether important decisions and emotional payoffs have enough time, whether actions fit their beats, and whether dialogue repeats the picture. If shortcomings exist, propose the smallest beat-specific revision. The gate does not grant approval.
6. Return `PROPOSED` screenplay with beats, revision, assumptions, unanswered questions and changed IDs. Director handles user approval.

Never claim generated footage or imply permission for a video-generation service. Consult [narrative craft](references/guide.md) when a deeper beat or narration audit is needed.

## Story craft — use when beats or emotional direction need strengthening

Create **filmable drama**, not generic plot summaries. Look for a visible protagonist objective, a meaningful change, an earned payoff and an image that transforms the opening. Prefer picture and action over redundant narration; show why the story choice suits the requested runtime, genre and audience. Challenge weak beats constructively, but respect unconventional, lyrical or comedic forms. Mark all draft material PROPOSED and never revise approved wording silently. Read [short-film craft and worked examples](references/short-film-craft.md) when proposing, improving or critiquing a screenplay, especially for emotional arcs, narrative pacing and dialogue restraint.

## Detailed reference

For a screenplay pre-handoff review or a reported pacing defect, read [Creative Quality Gate](references/creative-quality-gate.md), and use the optional offline checklist in `docs/storytelling-quality-gate.md` if repository scripts are accessible. When refining premise, character motivation, beat timing, filmable action or narration, read [storytelling principles](references/storytelling.md) and [narrative craft](references/guide.md). If the user provided a personal biography, never invent missing facts or assume consent to disclose them.
